# Performance round results, 2026-09-29

Measured with `examples/perf.rs` in release builds, back to back on an otherwise idle machine:
`before` is the round's base (`cbe4e98` plus the example), `after` is `perf-round` with every
track merged. 500 objects, 20 000 activities, 2 000 photos; median of 20 runs (3 for export and
import), in milliseconds. The raw numbers are in `before-2026-09-29-*.json` and
`after-2026-09-29-*.json`; `cargo run --release --example perf -- --compare a.json b.json`
prints the full table. Differences under about 15% on endpoints of a few milliseconds are within
run-to-run noise.

The `baseline-2026-09-29-*.json` files are the first baseline, taken while other builds loaded
the machine; the `before` files replace them for comparison.

Not improved, and why:

- PostgreSQL's timeline first page is about 1.7 ms slower (6.0 ms). Its queries are the same as
  before the round; SQLite's got faster. Not chased further at this size.
- Import is still the slowest thing by far. Storing the archive's files (hash, thumbnail, two
  fsyncs each) was 14.7 of SQLite's 19 s and now runs four at a time; the rows are about 4 s of
  one `record_create` per row, which a later round could batch.
- `/stats` (about 35 ms on SQLite), tags (29 ms) and the sync bootstrap (0.4–0.6 s for 1.5 MB)
  were outside this round's scope.
